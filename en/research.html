FAQ

How is an AI voice agent different from a push-button IVR?

An IVR makes callers press 1 or 2 through a menu, and one wrong turn means starting over. MosaTalk listens to what the caller says and answers in natural language — about one second after they stop talking — and callers can interrupt at any time.

Can the AI answer our phones, including our existing support line?

Yes. Customers can call from a web page in any modern browser that supports WebRTC, with nothing to install. Through a telephony provider integration, customers can also dial your existing support number from a landline or mobile and the AI answers.

Does it understand Taigi? Can it reply in Taigi?

It understands Taigi, including natural Taigi–Mandarin code-switching — the model is optimized for Taiwanese accents and Taigi. Taigi speech synthesis isn't available yet, so for now it understands Taigi and replies in Mandarin. Taigi synthesis and Hakka support are still in development, not deliverable features.

Are call recordings uploaded to the cloud?

No. Recognition, dialogue and speech synthesis all run on your own servers, with no calls to any cloud AI service. Standalone mode needs only your intranet; edge-split mode also needs one small cloud host.

What hardware do we need?

Ubuntu Linux x86_64 with one NVIDIA GPU with plenty of memory; it's validated on a single 46 GB card. We can supply the hardware and install it on site.

Does updating the knowledge base require coding?

No — a Q&A-format document is enough. When a question isn't covered by your material, the AI doesn't make up an answer; it asks the caller to leave their contact details.

FAQ

What is AI meeting transcription?

It turns meeting recordings into a transcript automatically, identifies who said what, and writes a summary and action items. MosaMinutes lets you edit while you listen in the same screen, until it's ready to send.

Can it transcribe recordings we already have?

Yes. Besides live recording, you can upload existing audio files; uploads go through a batch queue that isn't limited by the number of live streams. The transcript is punctuated, split into paragraphs and labeled by speaker, with a summary and action items.

Can meeting audio stay completely off the cloud?

Yes — that's the default, not an add-on. Recording, transcription, summaries and action items all happen on your own servers. It's containerized, runs on a single machine, and works on air-gapped networks.

Does it handle meetings in Taigi and Hakka?

Mandarin, Taigi, Hakka, Indigenous languages and English are all transcribed into Traditional Chinese with automatic punctuation. Hakka covers all six accents: Sixian, Hailu, Dapu, Raoping, Zhao'an and South Sixian.

Can it connect directly to Google Meet, Microsoft Teams or Zoom?

Not yet. Today you record or upload audio. Capturing online-meeting audio directly from your computer (without a bot joining the call) is on the roadmap, not a deliverable feature.

How many meetings can one machine transcribe at once?

One NVIDIA DGX Spark GB10 handles 10 live transcription streams; uploads go through a batch queue with no stream limit. Beyond one machine's capacity, it scales out to multiple machines.

If several people proofread at once, will edits get overwritten?

No. Proofreading runs on four independent channels that never overwrite each other. When there's a real conflict, the system doesn't silently pick a side — it flags the exact sentence.

PrinciplesA line we drew ourselves

Emotion inference is for customers only

Scoring contact-center agents is workplace monitoring, and emotion inference is its most contested form — the EU has banned emotion recognition in the workplace. Our principle: emotion inference applies only to customers; agent scoring is based on auditable, objective behavior and is never the sole basis for performance reviews. We drew this line at the design stage, not after a customer asked.

Open standards, no lock-in

Built on open-source models and standard compatible interfaces, so models can be swapped or upgraded. Where government or financial customers need a model set that meets supply-chain origin rules, we evaluate and swap in open-weight models from other sources, then re-validate quality.

What can call quality analytics actually do?

Customer-service and telesales calls that used to be 1–3% spot-checked become searchable data: every call heard in full, transcribed line by line, tagged with emotion and a 0–100 purchase-intent score, with a reason for every score change. It currently requires dual-channel recordings.

How accurate are the scores?

The scores haven't been validated against real sales outcomes, so we publish no accuracy figures. Validation is the first phase of deployment: we test against your own recordings and actual results before anything else.

Do recordings need to go to the cloud?

No. The system runs on a Linux GPU server in your server room; recordings and transcripts never leave it.

How is this different from a manager spot-checking calls?

Spot checks see a sample; this sees every call. And every judgment is checkable on the spot: utterance-level playback plus per-channel subtitles, with dual-channel separation so speaker labels are correct without guessing.

Every industry talks differently. Does that require code changes?

No. Each industry gets its own domain dictionary — swap the dictionary, not the code.

FAQFAQ

What is on-prem AI? Is it the same as private cloud or local deployment?

In Taiwan's procurement language, on-prem, local, private and intranet deployment usually mean the same thing: models and data live on machines your organization controls, not in a vendor's cloud. "Private cloud" is broader — sometimes your own data center, sometimes a rented dedicated cloud environment. Ours is the former: physical machines on your premises, with no internet required.

Does it work on networks with no internet access at all?

Yes. It's containerized and runs on a single machine, completing recognition, summaries and speech synthesis without going online. The exception is MosaTalk's edge-split mode, which needs one small cloud host; standalone mode needs only your intranet.

Is on-prem always more expensive?

The upfront cost is higher — cloud services work as soon as you sign up, while on-prem needs servers and setup. The difference comes later: cloud subscriptions charge by seat or by hour, so the more you use the more you pay; on-prem is built once with no per-call API fees. The answer depends on your volume and how many years you'll use it, not the first invoice. We can run the numbers with your actual meeting or call volume.

Can the models be tuned to our terminology?

Yes. Our speech recognition models are trained on architectures we built ourselves rather than by adjusting a generic model's parameters, so they can be deeply customized for your domain terms, recording conditions and acoustics. MosaScore also uses one domain dictionary per industry — swap the dictionary, no code changes.

Our procurement rules specify model origin. Can you meet that?

Yes. Model sources and the supply chain can be specified to match your procurement rules. That's a structural difference from cloud services, where the buyer usually has no control over the vendor's model stack.

Is keeping audio off the cloud a legal requirement, or an internal policy?

We've seen both, and they come from different places. Personal data and medical records are governed by Taiwan's Personal Data Protection Act; government agencies also have the Cyber Security Management Act and its security-level requirements; financial institutions follow their regulator's internal-control and audit rules. Even more common is a third case: no law forbids the cloud, but internal policy or a client contract says data may not leave your environment. We don't decide which applies to you — that's for your compliance and security teams. Our job is to make "not in the cloud" technically real, and to adjust access control, audit logs and retention to your actual requirements.

What is your recognition accuracy?

We don't give marketing numbers. Character error rates, competitor comparisons, test methodology and raw audio will be published in full to academic standards, and we can evaluate on public benchmarks you choose or your own audio. Until then, the most meaningful number is the one measured on your own recordings in a proof of concept.

FAQFAQ

Can Taigi recordings be transcribed?

Yes, including recordings that mix Taigi and Mandarin. MosaMinutes transcribes Taigi into a Traditional Chinese transcript with automatic punctuation and speaker labels, all on your own machines.

Which Hakka accents does transcription support?

All six: Sixian, Hailu, Dapu, Raoping, Zhao'an and South Sixian, for both recognition and synthesis. The classification follows the Hakka Affairs Council's six accents and can be checked item by item.

What about Indigenous languages?

MosaMinutes supports 42 languages across 16 Indigenous peoples. Our team built these models in a long-term partnership with iThuan, and they're public on Hugging Face for anyone to download and check. The voice agent doesn't support Indigenous languages yet.

How is this different from a general-purpose speech recognition service?

General services usually add local-language support by fine-tuning an existing model, and accuracy drops as accents and code-switching pile up. Our models are trained on architectures we built ourselves, so they can be customized for specific accents, terminology and recording conditions. The other difference is deployment: local-language recordings are often the most sensitive, and general services all run in the cloud.

What is the recognition accuracy?

We don't give marketing numbers. Accuracy for local languages depends heavily on accent, recording conditions and how much code-switching there is, so a percentage without a test set means nothing.
